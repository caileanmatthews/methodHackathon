**INTERVIEWER:** Gary, what equipment in the Valley District worries you most?

**GARY:** The big Allied oil breakers on the 115 kV yard at Millbrook. The BOC-115s. Those have the pneumatic mechanism, the air-operated one, not the spring kind. They've got their own compressor and air tank in the cabinet.

**INTERVIEWER:** What goes wrong with them?

**GARY:** Cold weather. Every winter. Moisture gets into the air system, and when it gets real cold, it freezes in the valves and the lines. Then the breaker's slow to trip or it goes into low-pressure lockout and won't operate at all.

**INTERVIEWER:** What's the fix?

**GARY:** Drain the air tank. Every week in winter, more if it's wet. Most guys skip it. Check the air dryer, check the compressor heater. If a valve's frozen, you thaw it, you don't beat on it. And if the pressure gauge is reading low, believe it. Don't let ops force an operation on a low-pressure breaker.

**INTERVIEWER:** Does that apply to all the Allied breakers?

**GARY:** I only ever worked the 115s. The 69 kV ones over in Ridge District are a different animal, I think those are spring mechanisms. Ask the Ridge guys.
