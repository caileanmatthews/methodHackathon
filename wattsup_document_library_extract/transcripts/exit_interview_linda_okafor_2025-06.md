**INTERVIEWER:** Linda, what's the one thing in the Ridge District you're most worried about after you leave?

**LINDA:** Quarry Road. Feeder 3. Those old Keystone electromechanical relays. They're fine relays, I've tested them for twenty-some years. But that station sits right next to the limestone quarry, and the dust gets into everything.

**INTERVIEWER:** What does the dust do?

**LINDA:** These are induction disc relays. There's an aluminum disc that spins, and a permanent magnet, the drag magnet, that slows it down. That magnet has a tiny air gap. Fine grit and any little iron filings from the crushing equipment get pulled into that gap and it starts to rub on the disc. When the disc drags, the relay picks up high and it trips slow. It still works, so nobody notices, until you have a fault and it takes way longer to clear than it should.

**INTERVIEWER:** How would someone spot it?

**LINDA:** On the test, pickup comes in high. Spec is plus or minus five percent of tap. If you've got a 5 amp tap and it's picking up at 5.3 or 5.5, that's your sign. Timing will be slow too, outside the seven percent. Or after a real fault, look at the event recorder and compare how long the relay took to what the curve says it should take. If it's way long, it's dragging.

**INTERVIEWER:** And the fix?

**LINDA:** First, isolate it properly. Open the trip paddles first, then the current paddles. Always that order. The current paddles short the CT before they open. You never, ever want an open CT secondary with the feeder in service. People get hurt that way.

Then take the cover off, look in the magnet gap with a flashlight. Use the magnet gap cleaner, it's a thin brass strip. Run it through the gap and wipe it off, run it through again until it comes out clean. Do not blow it out with compressed air. I've seen guys do that. It just packs the stuff in deeper and now you've got a real problem.

Spin the disc with your finger. It should move smooth and coast back to the stop. If it scrapes anywhere, you're not done. If the contacts look dirty, burnishing tool only. Never a file.

**INTERVIEWER:** And then?

**LINDA:** Retest. Pickup, and timing at a couple multiples. Two, five, ten times tap. Compare to the curve in the leaflet. Then check the cover gasket. On these, the gasket dries out and cracks and that's how the dust gets in. If it's cracked, replace it, or you'll be back in a year. That page in the leaflet about dusty locations is half covered in coffee, but that's what it says.

**INTERVIEWER:** How often should these be tested?

**LINDA:** At Quarry Road, every two years. Everywhere else the standard interval is fine. I wrote that recommendation on the work order in 2018. As far as I know it never made it into the schedule. Last time I checked, they were still on the regular cycle out there, and I'm not sure the last test even opened the cover.

**INTERVIEWER:** Anything else people should know?

**LINDA:** Check all three phases, not just the one that acted up. If one is full of grit, the other two are usually close behind. And the ground relay is in the same cabinet, so look at it while you're there.
