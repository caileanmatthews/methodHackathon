# Keystone Relay Company — Instruction Leaflet IL-KIO-8 (1986)
## Type KIO-8 Inverse Time Overcurrent Relay (Induction Disc)

> Photocopy from the Quarry Road relay cabinet binder. Page 7 has coffee damage; partially legible.

---

### Page 1 — Application
The KIO-8 is a single-phase, induction-disc inverse time overcurrent relay. Current in the operating coil produces torque on an aluminum disc. A permanent **drag magnet** provides damping and sets the disc speed. When the disc rotates to the contact-closed position, the relay closes its trip contacts and drops the operation target.

---

### Page 2 — Settings
- **Tap (pickup):** 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 10, 12 A secondary.
- **Time dial:** 0.5 to 11.

---

### Page 3 — Time–Current Characteristic
Operating time follows the inverse curve:

t = TD × ( 5.95 / (M² − 1) + 0.18 ) seconds

where TD is the time dial setting and M is the multiple of tap (relay current ÷ tap setting).

Representative values at **Time Dial 4.0**:

| Multiple of tap (M) | Operating time (s) |
|---|---|
| 2 | 8.65 |
| 3 | 3.70 |
| 5 | 1.71 |
| 10 | 0.96 |

---

### Page 4 — Test Tolerances
- **Pickup:** within ±5% of tap value. Pickup is the minimum current at which the disc starts and continues to rotate to contact closure.
- **Operating time:** within ±7% of curve value at multiples of 2 and above.
- The disc must return to the reset stop freely when current is removed.

---

### Page 5 — Removing the Relay from Service (Test Switches)
The relay is connected through a test switch assembly. To isolate the relay for testing:

1. Open the **trip circuit** paddles FIRST, so the relay cannot trip the breaker during testing.
2. Then open the **current (CT) paddles**. These are shorting-type switches that short the CT secondary before opening the relay circuit.

**DANGER:** Never open a CT secondary circuit while the primary is energized. Dangerous high voltage will develop across the open circuit. Restore in reverse order: current paddles first, verify no trip output, then trip paddles.

---

### Page 6 — Maintenance
- Inspect the **drag magnet air gap** and the disc. Foreign material, especially magnetic particles and fine dust, collects in the drag magnet gap and causes the disc to drag. A dragging disc increases both pickup and operating time.
- Remove material from the magnet gap with a **magnet gap cleaning tool** (thin non-magnetic strip). **Do not use compressed air**; it drives particles deeper into the gap.
- Rotate the disc by hand. It should move freely with no rubbing and coast back to the reset stop.
- Clean contacts only with a relay contact **burnishing tool**. Never use a file, sandpaper, or emery cloth.
- After any cleaning, retest pickup and operating time.
