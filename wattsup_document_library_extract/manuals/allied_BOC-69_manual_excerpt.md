### Page 1-2 — Safety

**WARNING — STORED ENERGY.** The SM-3 mechanism stores energy in its closing and opening springs. Before any work on the operating mechanism, trip latch, or linkage:

1. Breaker must be OPEN and isolated from the system by its disconnect switches, and grounded in accordance with the user's switching and grounding procedures.
2. Remove control power (open DC control circuit and motor circuit fuses or breakers).
3. Discharge BOTH the closing spring and the opening spring using the procedure in Section 3-4. Verify spring indicators read DISCHARGED.

Never attempt to adjust or lubricate the trip latch with springs charged.

---

### Page 3-4 — Discharging Springs
With control power removed, operate the manual CLOSE lever, then the manual TRIP push-button. Confirm the spring indicator shows DISCHARGED and the position indicator shows OPEN.

---

### Pages 4-1 to 4-5 — Mechanism Description
The SM-3 is a motor-charged spring stored-energy mechanism. On a trip signal, the trip coil plunger strikes the trip latch, releasing the trip latch roller and allowing the opening spring to drive the contacts open. An auxiliary "a" contact (52a) in series with the trip coil opens as the breaker opens, de-energizing the trip coil.

The speed of the opening stroke depends on the trip latch releasing freely. Any increase in friction at the trip latch or roller increases the time from trip coil energization to contact part.

---

### Page 4-6 — Trip Latch Adjustment
Latch engagement (overlap of trip latch on roller) shall be **0.060 to 0.090 inch**. Measure with a feeler gauge with springs discharged.

Adjust using the latch engagement adjusting screw. **Tighten locknut after adjustment.** Insufficient engagement can cause the breaker to trip on vibration; excessive engagement increases the force required from the trip coil and slows tripping.

---

### Pages 4-7 to 4-8 — Lubrication
Lubricate trip latch, latch roller, and pivot pins every 5 years or 2,000 operations, whichever comes first.

Use Allied grease specification **ASC G-11** only. Do not mix greases. Remove old grease with an approved solvent before re-lubricating. Apply a thin film; excess grease collects dirt.

### Page 4-12 — Cabinet Heaters
Two 250 W strip heaters are provided. One heater is connected for continuous operation to prevent condensation. The second is controlled by a thermostat. **Heaters must be energized at all times, including in summer.** For operation in ambient temperatures below 0°F (-18°C), refer to Section 4-13, Cold Weather Operation.

### Pages 6-1 to 6-4 — Tests and Acceptance Values

| Test | Acceptance value |
|---|---|
| Opening time (trip coil energized to contact part), rated control voltage | 3.0 cycles (50 ms) maximum at 60 Hz |
| Interrupting time (rated) | 5 cycles |
| Trip coil operating voltage range, 125 VDC nominal | Must trip reliably from 70 to 140 VDC |
| Trip coil resistance at 77°F (25°C) | 12.5 ohms ±10% |
| Contact resistance, per pole (DC, 100 A minimum test current) | 300 micro-ohms maximum |
| Insulating oil dielectric strength, ASTM D877, in service | 26 kV minimum (new oil: 30 kV minimum) |

**CAUTION:** Trip coils are rated for momentary duty only. The 52a contact must interrupt trip coil current. If the breaker fails to open, the trip coil remains energized and will overheat and fail within seconds to minutes. Do not apply repeated trip signals to a breaker that has failed to open; investigate the cause first.
